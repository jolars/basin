# Policy and timing pilot

The pilot finds useful reductions in work from alternative stopping checks, but
does not select defaults or freeze time budgets. Instrumentation perturbs the
cheap fixtures enough to matter, and some failures persist when approximate
stopping checks are disabled. The [manifest](2026-10-02-policies-003.toml),
[per-case results](2026-10-02-policies-003.results.csv), and [NIST target
audit](2026-10-02-policies-003.targets.csv) preserve the evidence.

## Scope and reproduction

The executable was built from `571235f`, before measurement. It ran serially on
logical CPU 2 of an Intel Core Ultra 7 155U, with single-thread settings and no
concurrent compilation. Ordinary desktop services remained active. This is a
development-machine pilot, not a controlled hardware benchmark.

Four development families supplied one case each: Misra1a, Chwirut2, DanWood,
and the rotated five-dimensional quadratic with Hessian condition number `1e8`.
Each has two starts and native `f32`/`f64` evaluation. The four solvers
therefore have 16 matched cases each. Holdout families were rejected by the
runner and were never optimized.

  | Solver      | Default                             | Probe (`f64`; `f32`)                                                                | Strict control                       |
  | ----------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
  | Nelder–Mead | No optional approximate checks      | Absolute simplex size AND cost spread: `1e-4`; `1e-3`                               | Same as default                      |
  | L-BFGS-B    | Absolute projected gradient `1e-10` | Projected gradient `1e-5`; `1e-3`                                                   | Projected-gradient check disabled    |
  | Nielsen LM  | Absolute gradient `1e-8`            | Orthogonality OR paired model reduction: `1e-8`; `1e-4`, absolute gradient disabled | Optional approximate checks disabled |
  | TRF         | Absolute scaled gradient `1e-8`     | Scaled gradient `1e-6`; `1e-4`                                                      | Scaled-gradient check disabled       |

All policies retain algorithm controls and numerical safeguards. A strict
control restarts from the same input; it does not resume a stopped solver. The
probes exercise existing APIs and do not implement the complete reference
policies in [candidate-pilot.md](../candidate-pilot.md).

There are 4,800 retained solves and an equal number of discarded warmups: 4,032
timing controls, 192 detailed policy traces, and 576 time-budget runs. The first
two phases use a 2,000-iteration cap with no time/pass cap. Controls use seven
alternating blocks of plain, final-only, and full-trace execution. Every mode
and repetition has identical returned parameters, authoritative counts,
iterations, and stopping evidence in all 192 policy/case comparisons.

The serial driver records exact commands, compiler, binary and lockfile hashes,
affinity, thread settings, timestamps, and process snapshots. Its manifests and
raw output live under `target/convergence-defaults/2026-10-02-policies-003/`.
The checked-in [source hashes](2026-10-02-policies-003.sources.sha256) and
[output hashes](2026-10-02-policies-003.outputs.sha256) identify the inputs and
records. The manifest supplies all reproduction commands.

## Recording costs

Each table entry aggregates 48 matched comparisons: 16 cases and three policies.
First take the median of seven repetitions for each mode, then form the
trace/plain ratio. Brackets show the middle 50% of these paired ratios; they are
descriptive spread, not confidence intervals. Per-case plain timing quartiles
remain in the results CSV.

  | Solver      | Charged solve-time ratio, median [Q1, Q3] | Whole harness ratio, median |
  | ----------- | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
  | Nelder–Mead | 1.197 [1.102, 1.299]                      | 3.185                       |
  | L-BFGS-B    | 1.085 [1.063, 1.127]                      | 1.471                       |
  | Nielsen LM  | 1.154 [1.110, 1.219]                      | 2.072                       |
  | TRF         | 1.012 [0.994, 1.040]                      | 1.138                       |

Plain mode disables fixture recording and times the whole ordinary Executor.
Recorded modes sum initialization and individual step durations, excluding
diagnostics and trace construction. The charged ratio measures perturbation
under these different timing boundaries, not the entire cost of tracing. The
whole harness measurement includes diagnostic and trace construction work but
excludes CSV formatting and fixture/solver construction in every mode. A TRF
ratio near one is inconclusive as an overhead estimate.

Median plain solve times range from 2.267 microseconds to 15.388 milliseconds.
These cheap cases expose clock, bookkeeping, allocation, and cache effects. Do
not subtract one global overhead percentage, rank solvers by the recorded times,
or freeze a common calibration budget from this pilot. Plain policy timings and
separate physical pass counts are the firmer comparisons.

## Stopping behavior and quality

Nelder–Mead's default reaches the 2,000-iteration cap in all 16 cases. The probe
stops through its paired checks in all 16, with a median of 65 iterations and
122 value passes, versus 7,923 value passes for the default. The returned
relative objective gap is at most `5.17e-6` for `f32` and `5.77e-8` for `f64`.
The stricter/default runs produce smaller gaps. The `f64` probe misses the
proposed `1e-8` target on Chwirut2 start 2 and the quadratic start 2; this is an
observed accuracy/work tradeoff, not proof of false convergence. Test nearby
paired thresholds before selecting a policy.

For L-BFGS-B in `f64`, the probe reduces median derivative passes from 2,036 to 67.
It has six projected-gradient stops and two iteration limits; the default has
two projected-gradient stops, four limits, and two failures. The probe's worst
relative gap is `3.39e-11`. In `f32`, Misra1a remains at relative gaps of
approximately `0.00180` and `0.00350` at both starts under every policy. Giving
the same stalled trajectory stricter stopping tolerances does not resolve that
result.

The LM probe produces native convergence reports in all eight `f64` cases,
compared with four convergence and four no-progress reports for the default.
Median value/derivative passes fall from `15 / 11.5` to `10 / 8`; the worst
relative gap is about `9.87e-14`. In `f32`, the probe has seven convergence
reports and one no-progress report, with worst gap `7.96e-8`. These are evidence
for investigating the native LM checks further, not a claim that every changed
termination label is more accurate.

TRF's probe changes little in these cases. The default and probe each have three
`f64` failures and five `f32` failures. In `f32`, both Misra1a starts fail to
construct or solve a finite feasible trust-region model at the same poor gaps
seen with L-BFGS-B. Other failures occur near the certified minimum. Strict
controls retain these failure paths. Keep solver failure, attained accuracy, and
budget exhaustion separate; a failure near a solution does not establish that
the solver reported convergence.

## Reference checks and attainable targets

Fifty of the 192 final records have a negative signed objective difference. An
independent Python audit reevaluates the 144 NIST returned points from the
decimal source observations, using exact binary returned parameters and both 60-
and 90-digit arithmetic. The two precisions agree within `1e-50` relative to the
larger of one and the objective. All negative differences fit within the
half-RSS certificate's printed rounding interval.

  | Case     | Half-RSS rounding half-width | Largest difference between binary and decimal reevaluation |
  | -------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
  | Misra1a  | `2.5e-12`                    | `6.43e-14`                                                 |
  | Chwirut2 | `2.5e-9`                     | `2.71e-13`                                                 |
  | DanWood  | `2.5e-14`                    | `1.78e-16`                                                 |

For each NIST case, start, and precision, at least one strict solver run
demonstrates every proposed target: all 42 combinations. The audit uses the
upper objective gap and lower initial gap induced by the certificate rounding
interval, and reevaluates the rounded starting point at high precision. It
retains the witnessing solver and numerical margin in the target CSV. This
establishes attainable targets for this small subset under the printed
certificate uncertainty; it is not a general precision-floor certification or a
solver success-rate denominator. The quadratic and remaining families still need
their own audit. The trace's 646 observed grid crossings remain unscored, and
negative differences are retained rather than silently clamped.

## Common boundary budgets

After inspecting the controls, the manifest recorded `0.1`, `1`, and `10` ms as
a common logarithmic diagnostic range. Every solver used its strict policy,
three repetitions, and a 100,000-iteration safety cap. No run reached that
safety cap. Longer runs remain censored by their time budget; this range does
not establish a sufficient upper budget for calibration.

  | Allowance | Runs | Time-limit flags | Median overshoot among flagged runs | Largest overshoot |
  | --------- | ---- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------- |
  | 0.1 ms    | 192  | 111              | 0.937%                              | 3.982 ms          |
  | 1 ms      | 192  | 84               | 0.0522%                             | 0.0120 ms         |
  | 10 ms     | 192  | 84               | 0.00491%                            | 0.00654 ms        |

Three 0.1 ms runs retain a native stop while also recording a time-limit flag.
The largest overshoot is one Nelder–Mead `f32` Misra1a run: its charged duration
is 4.082 ms, including one 4.022 ms segment. The cause of this isolated timing
outlier was not profiled; do not attribute it to solver work. Every budget run
has an earlier published boundary within the allowance. Analysis retains that
point separately and never counts the overshooting returned point as attained
within budget. Even without that outlier, some TRF boundaries exceed 0.1 ms by
roughly 30%, so observation granularity matters at short budgets.

The last point published within each allowance gives the following worst
relative gap across the four cases, both starts, and three repetitions. A solver
that has already stopped retains its terminal point. These are descriptive
quality measurements under the instrumented clock, not a solver ranking or a
target success score.

  | Solver      | Precision | 0.1 ms     | 1 ms       | 10 ms      |
  | ----------- | --------- | ---------- | ---------- | ---------- |
  | Nelder–Mead | `f32`     | `5.17e-6`  | `2.34e-8`  | `2.34e-8`  |
  | Nelder–Mead | `f64`     | `3.05e-4`  | `9.84e-14` | `9.84e-14` |
  | L-BFGS-B    | `f32`     | `3.50e-3`  | `3.50e-3`  | `3.50e-3`  |
  | L-BFGS-B    | `f64`     | `3.50e-3`  | `9.87e-14` | `9.87e-14` |
  | Nielsen LM  | `f32`     | `3.14e-9`  | `3.14e-9`  | `3.14e-9`  |
  | Nielsen LM  | `f64`     | `9.85e-14` | `9.85e-14` | `9.85e-14` |
  | TRF         | `f32`     | `3.50e-3`  | `3.50e-3`  | `3.50e-3`  |
  | TRF         | `f64`     | `2.33e-2`  | `9.84e-14` | `9.84e-14` |

Increasing the allowance from 0.1 to 1 ms improves the worst observed `f64`
quality for three solvers. Increasing it to 10 ms does not change any solver's
worst gap in this subset. The persistent `f32` Misra1a result therefore needs
more than a larger common budget within the tested range.

## Consequences

Continue development without asking the maintainer to invent application weights
or a universal price for accuracy. The next timing control should use an
unrecorded boundary driver with a clock spanning initialization, retain physical
work in matched separate runs, and compare it with ordinary Executor execution.
Basin's existing `max_time` starts at its first post-initialization check, so
substituting it would change this protocol's measurement definition.

Extend the reference audit, test nearby Nelder–Mead and LM policies, and
investigate the persistent `f32` failure/stagnation cases before a frozen
calibration sweep. Keep the [coverage gaps](../coverage.md#before-calibration)
and solver-specific reference gates open. No production defaults changed, no
held-out results were consulted, and no further human decision is required by
this pilot.
