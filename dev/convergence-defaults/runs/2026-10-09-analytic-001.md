## Case and reference

One development-family fixture: `f(x) = 0.5*((x0-1)^2 + (x1+2)^2)`, start
`(4,3)`, bounds `[-8,8]`, coordinate scales 1, and objective scale 17. Its exact
dyadic optimum `(1,-2)` has objective and gradient zero in native `f64` and
`f32`; the binary preflights this witness before solves. Quality requires
objective, box stationarity, feasibility, and parameter accuracy together.
Designated targets are `1e-6` and `1e-3`, respectively. These analytic
certificates do not transfer to NIST or other models.

## Interpretation

1. Fusion matters: analytic L-BFGS-B makes two physical fused calls, producing
   two logical cost and two logical gradient requests. The logical sum of four
   is not physical work.
2. Adapter expansion matters: central differences use four cost probes per
   gradient request, so `3 + 4*3 = 15`. Forward differences use three, so
   `28 + 3*28 = 112`. Independent CSV checks reconcile these identities.
3. Termination codes and accuracy differ: forward differences return the exact
   analytic optimum despite a native line-search failure. Retain that failure
   rather than relabeling it convergence. Derivative error and attainable native
   stop thresholds still need independent preflight in the full pilot.
4. Work after attainment is visible: paired NM spends 51 additional f64 calls
   and 26 f32 calls after meeting the designated target. Default f64 NM spends
   5929 additional calls before the physical cap interrupts it. This is evidence
   that the trace distinguishes attainment from stopping, not sufficient
   evidence to adopt the paired thresholds.
5. Caps are exact: the interrupted NM runs have no physical overshoot. The
   logical wrapper records the denied request, explaining 6001 versus 6000 and 5
   versus 4. Initialization errors expose no public logical counts. The runner
   never invents a final returned checkpoint after a typed error.
